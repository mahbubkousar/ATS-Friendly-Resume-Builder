Understanding ATS

What is an Applicant Tracking System?

Learn how ATS works and why your resume needs to be optimized to get past automated screening

What is ATS?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by employers and recruiters to collect, scan, sort, and rank job applications. It acts as a digital gatekeeper, automatically filtering resumes before they reach human recruiters.

Critical Statistic:

Over 98% of Fortune 500 companies use ATS to screen resumes, and up to 75% of resumes are rejected before a human ever sees them.

How ATS Works

1

Resume Parsing

The ATS scans and extracts information from your resume (name, contact info, work experience, education, skills) and stores it in a structured database.

2

Keyword Matching

The system searches for specific keywords and phrases from the job description. Resumes are scored based on how well they match the required skills and qualifications.

3

Ranking & Filtering

Candidates are ranked based on their match score. Only the top-ranking resumes are passed to human recruiters for review.

4

Disqualification

Resumes with formatting issues, missing keywords, or parsing errors are automatically rejected, regardless of the candidate's actual qualifications.

ATS-Friendly Resume Requirements

Format & Structure

  • Use standard file formats (.docx or .pdf)
  • Simple, clean layout without complex designs
  • Standard fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman)
  • Font size between 10-12pt
  • Clear section headings

Content

  • Include relevant keywords from job description
  • Use standard section titles (Experience, Education)
  • Spell out acronyms (first mention)
  • Use action verbs and quantifiable achievements
  • Include both hard and soft skills

What to Avoid

  • Tables, text boxes, or columns
  • Graphics, images, or logos
  • Headers and footers (except name)
  • Fancy fonts or colors
  • Special characters or symbols

Technical Details

  • Use standard date formats (MM/YYYY)
  • Include phone number and email at the top
  • Use standard bullet points (•, -, or ·)
  • Left-align text for better parsing
  • Save with a clear filename (FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf)

Keyword Optimization Strategy

1. Analyze the Job Description

Read the job posting carefully and identify:

  • ▸ Required skills: Technical skills, software, tools, certifications
  • ▸ Action words: Managed, developed, implemented, led, analyzed
  • ▸ Industry terms: Specific jargon or terminology used in the field
  • ▸ Qualifications: Education level, years of experience, certifications

2. Integrate Keywords Naturally

Don't just list keywords—use them in context throughout your resume:

❌ Bad Example:

"Skills: Python, JavaScript, React, Node.js"

✅ Good Example:

"Developed full-stack web applications using React and Node.js, implementing RESTful APIs with Python and JavaScript"

3. Use Both Acronyms and Full Terms

Some ATS search for acronyms, others for full terms. Include both:

Example: "Bachelor of Science (BS) in Computer Science" or "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)"

4. Match Job Title & Level

If you're applying for a "Senior Software Engineer," use that exact title in your resume (if accurate).

Common ATS Mistakes That Reject Resumes

❌ Using Images for Text

ATS cannot read text embedded in images. Your name, contact info, and skills must be plain text.

❌ Creative Section Headers

Using "Where I've Been" instead of "Experience" confuses the ATS. Stick to standard titles.

❌ Incorrect File Type

Submitting .jpg, .png, or uncommon formats. Always use .docx or .pdf unless specified otherwise.

❌ Keyword Stuffing

Hiding keywords in white text or listing them unnaturally. Modern ATS can detect this.

❌ Missing Contact Information

Putting your email or phone in the header/footer where ATS can't parse it.

❌ Inconsistent Formatting

Different date formats, inconsistent spacing, or mixed fonts confuse the parser.
